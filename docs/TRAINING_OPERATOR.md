# PMD Operator Sheet — iPad Training

This is the master script for the training video and the printable quick
reference card. The content is written for operators 60+ who are new to
the iPad. Pacing is slow on purpose. Vocabulary is concrete (`tap`,
`type`, `the green button`, `the top of the screen`). The trainer reads
the **NARRATION** out loud while the screen recording plays. The
**ON-SCREEN TEXT** is the big-font overlay that follows the finger.

---

## How to record

- **Device**: a real iPad (10-inch or 11-inch), portrait orientation.
- **Recording**: iPad → Settings → Control Centre → add **Screen
  Recording**. Swipe down from the top right → tap the round record
  icon → 3-second countdown → start.
- **Voice-over**: hold the record icon, turn **Microphone** ON, then tap
  Start. Voice and screen go into the same file.
- **Finger pointer**: enable **AssistiveTouch** → **Settings →
  Accessibility → Touch → AssistiveTouch → Always Show Menu = OFF**, but
  enable **Highlight touches** in **iOS 17 → Settings → Accessibility →
  Touch → AssistiveTouch → Highlight Touches** so the trainer's finger
  appears as a circle on screen.
- **Speed**: speak slowly. Pause **2 seconds** after every action so the
  viewer can copy it.
- **Captioning**: after recording, drop the file in iMovie. Add the
  ON-SCREEN TEXT overlays from each scene below. Use **font size 60+**,
  white text on a dark background bar, **stay on screen ≥ 4 seconds**.
- **Duration target**: Part 1 ≈ 6 minutes. Part 2 ≈ 4 minutes. Total
  ≤ 10 minutes — anything longer and the room loses attention.

# Part 1 — Your Shift, Start to Finish

> Goal: the operator can fill in one full shift on their own after
> watching this once. Every shift looks the same; learn this sequence,
> and you are done.

## Scene 1 — Open the app

**SHOW**: iPad home screen. The Safari icon is highlighted.

**NARRATION**
> Welcome. This video shows you how to fill in your shift on the iPad.
> It takes about ten minutes to watch. After this video, you will be
> able to do the whole job on your own.
>
> First, find Safari on the home screen. Safari is the round blue icon
> with a compass. Tap it once.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> 1.  Tap **Safari**

**PAUSE 3 seconds**

---

## Scene 2 — Open PMD

**SHOW**: Safari opens. The trainer taps the **PMD** bookmark in the
favourites bar.

**NARRATION**
> The page you need is already saved. At the top of Safari you see a
> bar with bookmarks. Tap the one that says **PMD**. The page will
> open. Wait two seconds for it to load.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> 2.  Tap the **PMD** bookmark

**PAUSE 2 seconds**

---

## Scene 3 — Pick your machine

**SHOW**: The Operator sheet is open. The top bar has the date, the
shift tabs, and the Machine dropdown is highlighted.

**NARRATION**
> Look at the top of the screen. You see four boxes in a row. The first
> box, on the left, says **Machine**. Tap that box. A list opens. Find
> your machine — for example, 1600T or 550T — and tap it. The whole
> page now shows your machine.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> 3.  Tap **Machine** → pick your number

**PAUSE 3 seconds**

---

## Scene 4 — Pick your Job number

**SHOW**: The Job# field is highlighted. The trainer taps it and a
dropdown of jobs appears.

**NARRATION**
> Next box is **Job#**. This is your order number. Tap the box. A list
> drops down with every job that is currently open in the planning
> system. Find your job. Tap it. The Part number and the description
> will fill in automatically on the right.
>
> If your job is not in the list, that is fine. You can also type the
> number directly into the box. Use the keyboard. Then tap somewhere
> else to close the keyboard.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> 4.  Tap **Job#** → pick or type your job

**PAUSE 4 seconds**

---

## Scene 5 — Pick your name and your supervisor

**SHOW**: Operator dropdown highlighted, then Supervisor dropdown.

**NARRATION**
> Now find the **Operator** box. Tap it. Find your own name. Tap your
> name. Do the same for the **Supervisor** box. Pick the supervisor
> who is on shift with you.
>
> You only need to do this once at the start of the shift. The names
> stay until the next shift change.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> 5.  **Operator** = you
> 6.  **Supervisor** = your boss today

**PAUSE 3 seconds**

---

## Scene 6 — Type your starting counter

**SHOW**: The right side panel. Count Start field highlighted.

**NARRATION**
> Look at the right side of the screen. You see a box called
> **Count Start**. This is the number on your machine's counter when
> your shift begins. Tap the box, type the number, and tap somewhere
> else to close the keyboard. The number is saved automatically.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> 7.  **Count Start** = the meter at clock-in

**PAUSE 3 seconds**

---

## Scene 7 — Fill the status row, one cell at a time

**SHOW**: The grid. Trainer taps one half-hour cell. The status picker
opens with R, B, C, D, I, M, O, P, S buttons. Trainer picks R.

**NARRATION**
> Now look at the big grid in the middle of the screen. Each column is
> half an hour of your shift. The first column is the first half hour.
> The last column is the last half hour.
>
> The top row is called **Machine Status**. Every half hour, tap one
> cell and pick what was happening. A panel opens with nine letters:
> R, B, C, D, I, M, O, P, S. **R** is green and means the machine was
> running. **B** is red and means a breakdown.
>
> For now, tap the first cell. Pick **R**. The cell turns green. That
> half hour is now logged.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> 8.  Tap a status cell
> 9.  Pick a letter
>
> R = Running ✓
> B = Breakdown ✗

**PAUSE 4 seconds**

---

## Scene 8 — Fill many cells at once (hold and drag)

**SHOW**: Trainer presses and holds the first cell, drags across five
cells, lifts the finger. Picker opens. Trainer picks R. All five cells
turn green.

**NARRATION**
> If the machine was running the same way for several half hours, you
> do not need to tap each one. **Press and hold** the first cell, then
> **drag** your finger across the cells you want. When you lift your
> finger, the panel opens. Pick the letter once. All cells you dragged
> across get filled together.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> Quick fill: **press, drag, lift, pick**

**PAUSE 4 seconds**

---

## Scene 9 — When you pick Breakdown (B)

**SHOW**: Trainer taps a cell, picks B. A second panel opens with the
breakdown categories — Mechanical, Electrical, Hydraulic, etc.

**NARRATION**
> When you pick **B** for breakdown, a second panel opens. It asks
> what kind of breakdown. Pick a category. Then pick the specific
> cause. This helps the engineers know what to fix next time.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> B → pick category → pick cause

**PAUSE 4 seconds**

---

## Scene 10 — Log rejects

**SHOW**: Below the status row, rows for D01, D02, D03 reject codes.
Trainer taps a cell in the D01 row, types 3.

**NARRATION**
> Under the status row, you see more rows. Each row is a reject
> category. If three pieces were scrapped between, say, ten and ten
> thirty, find the right row, find the column for ten to ten thirty,
> tap it, and type the number three. The total reject count on the
> right updates by itself.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> 10. Reject? tap the right row + column → type the number

**PAUSE 4 seconds**

---

## Scene 11 — Hand-over notes

**SHOW**: The four textareas — People, Plant, Machine, Material — on
the right side. Trainer types in the Machine box.

**NARRATION**
> Near the end of your shift, scroll down on the right side. You see
> four boxes: **People**, **Plant**, **Machine**, and **Material**.
> Write a short note in each one for the next shift. For example,
> in the Machine box: "Robot arm slow, please check." Keep it short
> — one or two lines.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> 11. Hand-over: write short notes for the next shift

**PAUSE 4 seconds**

---

## Scene 12 — End of shift: Count End and Sign Off

**SHOW**: Count End field. Trainer types a number. Then taps the green
**Sign off & Save** button at the top right. A confirmation dialog
appears with the totals. Trainer taps **Confirm**.

**NARRATION**
> At the end of your shift, type the final counter into the **Count
> End** box on the right. The **Total Good** number appears
> automatically — it is Count End minus Count Start minus rejects.
>
> Now look at the top right of the screen. You see a big green button:
> **Sign Off and Save**. Tap it. A summary opens. Check the numbers.
> If they look right, tap **Confirm**. Your shift is saved. You are
> done.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> 12. **Count End** = the meter at clock-out
> 13. Tap green **Sign Off & Save** → **Confirm**
>
> Done ✓

**PAUSE 5 seconds**

# Part 2 — Three Useful Tricks

> Goal: small things that save time. Watch when you have a quiet
> moment. Not needed on day one.

## Trick 1 — Look at yesterday or last week

**SHOW**: The left-arrow next to the date at the top. Trainer taps it
twice. Then taps **Today** to jump back.

**NARRATION**
> The top of the screen has a date. To the left of the date is a back
> arrow. Tap it once to go back one day. Tap it again to go back
> another day. To come back to today, tap the **Today** button. While
> you are looking at an old day, you cannot change anything — the page
> is read-only.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> ◀ = yesterday   |   **Today** = back to now

**PAUSE 3 seconds**

---

## Trick 2 — Refresh after planning sends a new order

**SHOW**: The Refresh button at the top right.

**NARRATION**
> When the planning team adds a new job, it appears in your Job# list
> after a few minutes. If you cannot see it yet, tap the **Refresh**
> button at the top right of the screen. The list updates immediately.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> ⟳ Refresh = pull the newest jobs

**PAUSE 3 seconds**

---

## Trick 3 — Fix a mistake

**SHOW**: A cell that was filled with R. Trainer taps it again, picks
B instead.

**NARRATION**
> Made a mistake? No problem. Tap the cell again. The panel opens.
> Pick the correct letter. The new letter replaces the old one.
> Numbers work the same way — tap the box, delete what is wrong,
> type the right number.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
> Wrong letter? tap again → pick the right one

**PAUSE 3 seconds**

**NARRATION**
> That is everything you need. The whole shift is the same eleven

> Thank you for watching.

# Trainer's Notes (Do Not Read on Camera)

These are notes for whoever is making the video, not for the narration.

- **Show a real, named operator filling in a real shift.** Generic
  demos lose attention. Even fake numbers feel more concrete than
  "tap this".
- **Audio quality matters more than video quality.** A 720p screen
  recording with clear audio beats a 4K recording with echo. Record
  in the engineering office, not on the floor.
- **One take per scene.** If a tap is wrong, stop, re-record that
  scene only, splice in iMovie. Editing is faster than re-recording
  the whole thing.
- **Test on a 10-inch iPad.** Some labels look fine on a 13-inch but
  wrap on a 10-inch. The video has to look like what they actually
  see.
- **Show what a finished shift looks like at the end.** A green-filled
  grid plus a saved confirmation is the strongest "this is the goal"
  signal for someone who has never used the app.
- **Pin the card before training, not after.** Operators internalise
  what they can see. The card on the wall is half the training.
- **Plan a follow-up session a week later.** Day one is fire-hose. Day
  seven is when the real questions appear. 20 minutes, no slides,
  just answering whatever they tripped on.
